# Redesign Workflow

For redesigning or significantly improving an existing site or page, not building from scratch.

---

# Phase 1: Audit the Existing Design

Step 1 — Screenshot the current state (full page).
This is the before. Never start redesign without documenting the current state.

Step 2 — Run the frontend audit.
Use prompts/frontend_audit.md. Get a prioritized list of problems.

Step 3 — Categorize problems.
Structural: layout, hierarchy, section patterns.
Surface: color, typography, spacing.
Detail: hover states, transitions, micro-details.

Step 4 — Identify the highest-leverage problems.
What single change would have the most impact? Usually: hierarchy in the hero, typography contrast, or section spacing.

Step 5 — Decide what to keep.
Not everything needs to change. Identify: what is working, what has brand equity, what the user specifically wants to preserve.

---

# Phase 2: Direction Reset

Step 6 — Define the direction for the redesign.
Even if the product is unchanged, the redesign has a direction: "more premium," "more approachable," "more technically credible."

Step 7 — Find new references.
Do not redesign based on the existing design. Find 2-3 references that represent where the design should go, not where it is.

Step 8 — Define what specifically to borrow from each reference.
"From [site]: the typographic scale. From [site]: the section spacing approach. From [site]: the card elevation on hover."

---

# Phase 3: Section Priority List

Step 9 — Rank sections by redesign priority.
Which section is most broken? Which is most visible? Which has the highest business impact?

Typical ranking: hero first, final CTA second, features third, everything else in order.

Step 10 — Commit to section-by-section redesign.
Do not attempt a full-page redesign in one pass. One section at a time, fully completed before moving.

---

# Phase 4: Hero Redesign

Step 11 — Apply hero_design_workflow.md to the hero section specifically.
The hero is the highest-priority section in any redesign.

Step 12 — Do not move to the next section until the hero is complete.
"Complete" means: correct hierarchy, no anti-slop patterns, visual element present and purposeful, mobile working.

---

# Phase 5: Remaining Sections

Step 13 — Section-by-section redesign.
For each section: evaluate against the section pattern reference (section_patterns.md). Identify the gap. Make targeted changes.

Step 14 — Maintain consistency as you go.
As each section is redesigned, check that it feels like it belongs to the same page as previously redesigned sections. Consistency is achieved section by section.

Step 15 — After all sections are redesigned: compare the full page to the before screenshot.
Is it clearly better? Is it coherent? Does the emotional direction feel consistent from top to bottom?

---

# Phase 6: Differential Polish

Step 16 — Identify the highest-craft moments on the page.
These are the sections users will look at longest: hero, features, pricing. These get the most polish.

Step 17 — Apply detail polish to high-craft moments only.
Hover states, micro-transitions, scroll reveals. Not everything needs premium detail — focus on the highest-impact moments.

Step 18 — Run final_polish_checklist.md before shipping.

---

# Common Redesign Mistakes

Starting from scratch instead of redesigning: If the existing design has brand equity, build on it, do not erase it.
Trying to redesign everything at once: Section by section. Breadth before depth produces mediocre everything.
Not updating references: Using old references produces an old-feeling redesign. Find new references for the new direction.
Neglecting the final CTA section: The most-neglected section in redesigns. It always improves with attention.
