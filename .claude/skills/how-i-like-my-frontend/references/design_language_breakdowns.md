# Design Language Breakdowns

How premium brands construct their visual identity. Study the system, not just the surface.

---

# Linear

Core System:
Dark backgrounds (#0A0A0A range). Near-zero color except one muted accent. Typography as primary visual element. Spacing so generous it feels architectural.

What They Do:
- Text hierarchy through opacity, not size variation alone (three white opacity levels: 95%, 60%, 35%)
- Padding that doubles expectations (section padding 160px+ vertical)
- Borders at 8-10% white opacity — present but barely
- Animations that are 200ms and invisible after first use
- Grid: 12 columns, 32px gutters, consistent

What Makes It Premium:
The restraint. Every element that could be added has been questioned and often removed. What remains is necessary.

What to Steal:
The opacity-based text hierarchy. The willingness to use extreme padding. The conviction that less is always more.

---

# Stripe

Core System:
Light base with gradient accents that feel purposeful. Strong typographic hierarchy. Information density without clutter. Product UI embedded naturally in marketing context.

What They Do:
- Gradient backgrounds built from specific directional angles (not random radial gradients)
- Bento-style feature cards with genuine content variety
- Code snippets as visual elements (not just screenshots)
- Testimonials from recognizable names with specific outcomes
- Navigation: clean, minimal, active-state clear

What Makes It Premium:
The combination of complexity and clarity. The product is technically complex; the design makes it feel approachable without oversimplifying.

What to Steal:
How to embed product UI in marketing pages without it looking like a screenshot dump. The bento approach to feature variety.

---

# Vercel

Core System:
Maximum restraint on a dark base. Black backgrounds, white text at varying opacities, one muted accent (often white or very light grey). Typography carrying all the visual weight.

What They Do:
- Headlines at full confidence — large, heavy, direct statements
- Zero illustrative elements in hero — the copy and product do the work
- Navigation at zero visual weight — barely visible against the background
- Sections separated by space alone, no dividers or background changes
- Motion: very subtle scroll reveals only

What Makes It Premium:
The confidence to do almost nothing and let the product speak. This only works when the product has genuine credibility.

What to Steal:
Navigation that visually recedes. The courage to not add imagery. Section separation through whitespace alone.

---

# Raycast

Core System:
Dark luxury with one strong brand gradient (purple-to-orange, used sparingly). Product UI demonstrations throughout. Custom gradient mesh backgrounds.

What They Do:
- Gradient mesh backgrounds that feel material and unique
- Product UI shown in realistic context (keyboard, user interaction)
- Each section demonstrates a distinct product capability
- Motion: scroll-linked product UI demonstrations
- Custom cursor on desktop

What Makes It Premium:
The integration of product reality into the marketing. Users believe the product works because they can see it working.

What to Steal:
How to create gradient mesh backgrounds that feel designed rather than random. Scroll-linked product demonstrations.

---

# Clerk

Core System:
Dark technical with carefully calibrated gradient accents. Strong grid, clear section separation, code as visual element, product UI embedded.

What They Do:
- Gradient borders on hero cards (1px border with gradient)
- Code syntax highlighting used as a visual element
- Bento feature grid with genuine layout variety
- Pricing table with one clearly recommended plan
- Dark to light transition section mid-page

What Makes It Premium:
The polish consistency across every section. The same level of attention to detail applies to every card, every label, every spacing decision.

What to Steal:
Gradient border technique for featured cards. The dark-to-light mid-page transition.

---

# Apple (Product Pages)

Core System:
White or dark, extreme space, typographic scale used boldly, product photography as the only visual element.

What They Do:
- Headlines at 80-96px+ with weight 700
- Body text restrained to 19-21px with generous line height
- No decorative elements — product imagery fills all visual roles
- Scroll-linked product demonstrations that feel cinematic
- Section transitions through background color change

What Makes It Premium:
The space. Apple uses more whitespace than any other brand at scale. It signals confidence and exclusivity.

What to Steal:
The willingness to let a headline be the only visual element on a dark background. The typographic scale confidence. Using space as a luxury signal.
