# Visual Styles

---

# Dark Luxury

Characteristics:
- Near-black or very dark grey backgrounds (#0A0A0A to #1C1C1E)
- Minimal color — one restrained accent (often gold, warm white, or subtle blue)
- High-contrast white text at varying opacities
- Generous whitespace — more than feels natural
- Slow, deliberate motion if any
- Serif or premium sans-serif display type

Best For:
- High-end SaaS, fintech, security, analytics
- Products that want to communicate precision and exclusivity
- Developer-focused tools

Risk:
Difficult to differentiate from competitors in the same dark-luxury space. Requires strong original visual elements.

Reference brands: Linear, Vercel, Raycast (dark variant)

---

# Light Minimal

Characteristics:
- White or off-white backgrounds
- Subtle grey surfaces for contrast
- One saturated accent color
- Clean sans-serif typography
- Light, airy section spacing
- Motion: light and quick

Best For:
- B2B SaaS with broad audience
- Productivity tools
- Products where approachability is key

Risk:
Requires discipline to avoid looking generic. The minimal light system has the most competition.

Reference brands: Notion, Linear (light), Figma

---

# Dark Technical

Characteristics:
- Dark backgrounds with subtle grid or dot patterns
- Monospace or geometric sans-serif type
- Green, cyan, or amber accents (terminal color associations)
- Code snippets, terminal interfaces, or data visualizations as visual elements
- Purposeful, precise motion

Best For:
- Developer tools, CLIs, APIs, infrastructure products
- Security and monitoring platforms

Risk:
Can feel cold or inaccessible to non-technical buyers. Know your audience.

Reference brands: Warp, Railway, Supabase (dark variant)

---

# Bento / Structured Grid

Characteristics:
- White or light grey background
- Heavy use of bordered or surface-contrast cards
- Mixed content types in a grid (screenshots, text, numbers, UI)
- Clean, geometric type
- Restrained palette with one warm or saturated accent

Best For:
- Feature showcases
- Products with multiple distinct capabilities
- Modern SaaS that wants to feel organized and comprehensive

Reference brands: Apple product pages, Stripe

---

# Editorial / Typographic

Characteristics:
- Typography as the primary visual element
- Large display type at unusual scales
- Minimal or no imagery in hero sections
- Strong grid discipline
- Serif display type often paired with clean sans body

Best For:
- Design-forward products
- Content platforms
- Portfolio and agency sites
- Products where confidence is the primary brand signal

Risk:
Requires exceptional type choices and spacing. Typographic designs with poor type choices look worse than any other style.

Reference brands: New York Times, are.na, editorial publications

---

# Glassmorphism (Restrained)

Characteristics:
- Dark or gradient background behind frosted glass panels
- One or two glass surfaces maximum
- Blur backdrop behind key content cards
- Subtle border at 10-15% white opacity on glass edges
- Should feel premium, not like a Figma template

Best For:
- One accent section on an otherwise dark design
- Modal and overlay components
- Dashboard sidebar elements

When to avoid:
- As the primary design language for an entire page
- When every card and section uses it (loses meaning)

Risk:
The most overused effect in AI-generated design after purple gradients. Use maximally once per page, never as the primary design language.

---

# Brutalist / Anti-Design

Characteristics:
- Raw, unstyled-feeling layouts with deliberate crudeness
- Heavy, oversized typography
- Limited or harsh color use
- Minimal transitions or deliberately rough interactions
- Grid-breaking elements

Best For:
- Creative agencies
- Design communities
- Products where non-conformity is the brand identity

Risk:
Extremely difficult to execute well. Bad brutalism looks broken. Good brutalism looks intentional. The margin between them is thin.

---

# Warm Editorial

Characteristics:
- Off-white or cream backgrounds
- Warm neutrals (tans, warm greys, earthy tones)
- Serif display type
- Generous whitespace
- Photography with warm tones
- Slow, graceful motion or no motion

Best For:
- Consumer lifestyle products
- Health, wellness, food, craft products
- Creative tools positioned as warm and human

Risk:
Can read as too lifestyle or consumer for B2B contexts. Strong brand alignment required.
